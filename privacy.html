The short version

Daily meds keeps your records on your phone. There are no accounts, ads or analytics. The person who makes Daily meds never receives your medications, symptoms, food, moods, notes or anything else you log, and nothing is sold or shared.

The only things that leave your phone are a rough location for weather (if you turn weather on) and the ordinary requests any web page makes to load. Each is explained below.

What's stored on your phone

Everything you enter stays in the app's storage on this device:

  • Medications and supplements, and the doses you check off
  • Symptoms, with times and how strong they were
  • Meals, foods and restaurants
  • Moods, smells, other things you log, appointments and notes
  • The name you set in Settings, if any
  • Each day's weather, air quality and pollen, if weather is on
  • A rough position for each day (about 1 km) and its elevation, if Track location is on
  • Your Google Pollen API key, if you add one

What leaves your phone

Weather, air quality and elevation (optional)

Weather is off until you pick a location. Then the app sends your location, rounded to about 1 km, to Open-Meteo to get the weather, air quality and elevation. If you type a city instead, the city name is sent to Open-Meteo to look it up. None of your records are sent. See Open-Meteo's terms and privacy policy.

Location (optional)

Your phone asks your permission before the app can use your location. It's used only to get the weather and, if Track location is on, to save a rough position once a day on the phone.

Pollen (optional)

If you add your own Google Pollen API key, the app sends that key and your rough location to Google's Pollen API about once a day. Google's privacy policy applies to that request.

Loading the app

  • The app's font comes from Google Fonts, so Google sees your device's IP address when the font downloads. See the Google Fonts privacy FAQ.
  • The app is served from GitHub Pages, which logs the IP address of devices that load it, for security. See GitHub's privacy statement.
  • If you installed Daily meds from an app store, that store's own privacy policy covers what it collects, such as downloads.

Files you save and share

Save backup and the spreadsheet export save files to your phone's Downloads. They contain your records. If you send them to your doctor or upload them to an AI service or anywhere else, that person's or service's privacy practices apply from then on.

How long it's kept, and deleting it

  • Your records stay until you delete them. Medication check-offs older than about 400 days and pollen readings older than 365 days are removed automatically.
  • You can delete single items in the app.
  • Turning off Track location stops saving new positions.
  • To delete everything, uninstall the app, or clear this site's data in your browser's settings. Save a backup first if you might want your records back.

Children, changes and contact

Daily meds isn't meant for children under 13, and it doesn't collect personal information from anyone.

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new date.

Questions? Open an issue at github.com/matthettich/daily-meds. Issues are public, so please don't include health details.
